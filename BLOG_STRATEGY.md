# Aari Transactions · Blog Content Roadmap

**Authored by:** Marlenyi Paredes, founder of Aari Transactions.
**Target audience:** Florida real estate agents searching mid-deal for specific answers.
**Goal:** Build the SEO traffic engine that compounds. Capture high-intent agent searches that lead to TC submissions and membership signups.
**Locked:** 2026-05-12.

---

## Strategy at a glance

**Authority play, not volume play.** Google rewards real estate content authored by licensed professionals with verifiable credentials. Marlenyi's broker license is the trust signal. Every post is bylined and bio-stamped.

**Cluster, don't sprinkle.** Three pillar topics. Each pillar has 5-8 supporting posts that link inward. Google indexes the pillar as authoritative and pushes traffic across the cluster.

**Local-first secondary.** Lehigh Acres, Fort Myers, Cape Coral. Lower volume but higher conversion. These are where Aari ranks fastest because competition is thinner.

**Cadence:** One post every 2 weeks. 26 posts/year. After 12 months you have a 26-post library that compounds in search traffic for years.

**Tone:** Cattoni voice. Period stops. No fluff. Tactical, not theoretical. Written from active files.

**Format:** Long-form pillar posts at 1,800-2,500 words. Tactical posts at 1,200-1,500 words. Local posts at 800-1,200 words.

## The three pillars

### Pillar A · Florida contract mechanics
The foundation cluster. Agents Googling specific contract questions are at peak intent. They want answers, they want them now, and they're checking who said it. Marlenyi's broker authority dominates this category.

### Pillar B · Closing delays and prevention
The pain cluster. Every agent has lost commission to a closing that slipped. Posts in this cluster solve specific recurring problems and end with "want a second set of eyes on your next file?"

The hyperlocal cluster. Lower search volume per post, but the searcher is 10x more likely to convert because they're already operating in our service area.

## How the cluster strategy works

Each pillar post links down to 5-8 supporting posts. Each supporting post links back up to its pillar and laterally to 2-3 siblings. This creates a "topic graph" Google reads as expertise. The pillar pages rank for the broadest keywords (e.g. "florida effective date"). The supporting pages rank for long-tail variants (e.g. "florida effective date counter offer"). Together, the cluster captures 10x the traffic of any single post in isolation.

## Publishing rhythm

**Bi-weekly.** Two posts per month. Tuesdays at 9am ET (best email open day, and gives Google one week to index before next post drops).

**Quality > quantity.** Skipping a week is fine. Publishing a weak post is not. The bar: would Marlenyi, as the author, hand this to a paying client and feel good about it?

**Republish + refresh.** Every 6 months, audit the oldest 6 posts. Update for any FAR/BAR contract changes, market shifts, or new compliance rules. Bump `dateModified` in JSON-LD. Google treats refreshed posts as new content and re-promotes them in rankings.

## SEO best practices per post

Every post on aaritransactions.com/blog/ must include:

- **Title tag** ≤ 60 characters with primary keyword early
- **Meta description** ≤ 155 characters with the question the post answers
- **One H1** matching the title
- **Schema.org BlogPosting JSON-LD** with author name (Marlenyi Paredes)
- **Canonical URL** to prevent duplicate content issues
- **Open Graph + Twitter card meta** for shareability
- **Author byline + bio at end** for E-E-A-T trust signal
- **Internal links** to at least 2 other relevant posts in the same cluster
- **External authoritative link** if relevant (Florida DBPR, FREC, FAR/BAR original text)
- **CTA** at the end pointing to /index.html#apply or /book.html
- **Disclaimer** that this is educational, not legal advice
- **Image alt text** if any images are used

## What NOT to write about

Skip these categories. They don't compound and they pull focus.

- **Generic "first time homebuyer" content** &middot; saturated, low conversion for a TC business
- **News commentary on rates / Fed announcements** &middot; ephemeral, doesn't compound
- **"Top 10 listings in Lehigh Acres"** &middot; that's MLS content, not authority content
- **Anything that sounds like a press release** &middot; not what your audience is searching for
- **Anything making income claims** &middot; FREC compliance risk, no exceptions

## How long until SEO traffic compounds

Realistic expectations:

- **Months 1-3:** Posts get indexed. Traffic is near zero. This is normal.
- **Months 4-6:** First rankings appear on long-tail queries. Trickle of organic visits begin.
- **Months 7-12:** Pillar posts start ranking on the first page for medium-difficulty terms. Traffic compounds.
- **Year 2:** Top 3 positions on chosen long-tail keywords. Traffic supports a meaningful portion of file submissions.
- **Year 3+:** The library becomes a moat. Competitors would need years to replicate.

This is a slow burn that pays in compounding returns. The first post earns nothing for months. By year 2, that same post is generating leads on autopilot every week.

## Measurement

Pull these monthly from Google Search Console + Plausible/GA4:

- **Impressions** &middot; how often Aari posts appear in search
- **Click-through rate** &middot; how compelling the title + meta are
- **Avg position** &middot; ranking trajectory per keyword
- **Top queries** &middot; what's working, what to write more of
- **Page-to-CTA conversion** &middot; blog readers → /index.html#apply or /book.html visits

Threshold to keep posting: any post averaging > 50 monthly impressions after 6 months is working. Posts under that threshold get refreshed or merged into a stronger sibling.
