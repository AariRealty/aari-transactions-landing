Effective Date, straight from the contract

By Marlenyi Paredes · Florida Real Estate Broker, License BK3530153

The effective date starts every deadline on your file. Here is the exact language from each Florida contract and the paragraph it lives under. One difference decides it: delivery.

FAR/BAR Residential Contract Paragraph 3 · Time for Acceptance of Offer and Counter-Offers; Effective Date
¶3

"The Effective Date shall be the date when the last one of the Buyer and Seller has signed or initialed and delivered this offer or final counter-offer."

FAR/BAR Residential Contract for Sale and Purchase, Paragraph 3

Plain English: the last signature and delivery to the other side. The day it is delivered is your effective date.

FAR/BAR "AS IS" Contract Paragraph 3 · Same standard as the FAR/BAR
¶3

The AS IS Residential Contract uses the same Paragraph 3 definition: last party to sign or initial and deliver the offer or final counter-offer.

FAR/BAR "AS IS" Residential Contract for Sale and Purchase, Paragraph 3

Plain English: identical to the standard FAR/BAR. Signature and delivery. The trap on AS IS is downstream, in the inspection window, not in the date itself.

NABOR (Naples) Contract Paragraph 2 · Period of Offer and Counter-Offer; Effective Date
¶2

"The Effective Date of this Contract shall be the last date either SELLER or BUYER signs or initials this Contract... the latest date set forth on this Contract by either party's signature or initials shall be the Effective Date."

NABOR Sales Contract (Residential Improved Property), Paragraph 2

Plain English: the last signature or initial, period. No delivery required. Your clock starts the day it is signed, even if the executed copy has not gone back to the other side yet.

FAR/BAR & AS IS

Signed and delivered. Effective date is the delivery day.

NABOR

Signed only. Effective date is the last signature day.
